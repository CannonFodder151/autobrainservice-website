# Fuel Cost Per Km: Australian Guide 2026

Every time you fill up at the bowser, you're paying for kilometres you've already driven. But most Australian drivers have no idea what each kilometre actually costs them. We know what we pay per litre. We know roughly how many litres our tank holds. But when it comes to the actual fuel cost per km — the real price of every trip we make — most of us are guessing.

If you're trying to budget for car ownership, compare vehicles before buying, or understand whether your driving habits are quietly inflating your fuel bills, knowing your fuel cost per km is the single most useful number you can track. It turns vague anxiety about rising fuel prices into clear, actionable data. And once you know your number, you can start doing something about it.

## What fuel cost per km means

Your fuel cost per km is exactly what it sounds like — the dollar amount of fuel it takes to drive one kilometre. While litres per 100 kilometres (L/100km) tells you how thirsty your car is, it doesn't tell you what that thirst costs in real money. A car that uses 7.0 L/100km costs very different amounts to run depending on whether fuel is $1.80 or $2.40 per litre, and that difference is invisible in the L/100km figure alone.

Fuel cost per km bridges that gap. It's a simple, transparent metric that cuts through the marketing spin and puts a real price on every trip. Whether you're commuting to work, doing the school run, or heading on a weekend road trip, this number tells you what that driving actually costs in dollars at today's prices.

Understanding your fuel cost per km also makes it easier to evaluate car finance and ownership decisions. If you're choosing between two cars, the one with lower fuel costs might save you thousands of dollars over a five-year ownership period — money that doesn't show up in the monthly repayment figure but hits your budget every single week.

## How to calculate fuel cost per km

The formula is straightforward. Start with your fuel economy in litres per 100 kilometres (L/100km) and the current price per litre:

**Cost per km = (L/100km × Price per litre) ÷ 100**

If you want to work it out directly from your fuel receipts rather than relying on your car's trip computer — which is often optimistically calibrated and can overstate your economy by 10–20% — use this version:

**Cost per km = Total fuel spend ÷ Total kilometres driven**

Here's a practical example. If you spent $320 on fuel last month and drove 1,600 km, your fuel cost per km is $0.20. At $0.20/km, a 40 km round trip to visit family costs $8 in fuel alone. That kind of clarity makes budgeting and route decisions much easier.

If you're calculating from your L/100km figure instead, take a car that uses 8.0 L/100km at $2.00 per litre: (8.0 × 2.00) ÷ 100 = $0.16/km. A smaller car at 6.0 L/100km on the same fuel costs just $0.12/km. That four-cent difference sounds small, but over 15,000 km a year it's $600 in your pocket — or gone. When you're comparing vehicles or trying to decide whether to upgrade, that number is what really matters.

## Average fuel cost per km Australia

The number varies significantly depending on what you drive. Australia's most popular vehicles span a wide range of efficiencies, and fuel prices differ between capital cities and regional areas. Petrol prices in regional and remote Australia can be 20–30 cents per litre higher than in capital cities, which directly increases your fuel cost per km even if your car's fuel economy hasn't changed.

Based on 2026 data and an approximate national average of $1.90–$2.10 per litre for unleaded 91:

| Vehicle | Fuel type | L/100km | Fuel cost per km |
|---|---|---|---|
| Toyota Corolla | Petrol | 6.5–7.5 | $0.12–$0.16 |
| Mazda CX-5 | Petrol | 8.0–9.5 | $0.15–$0.20 |
| Ford Ranger | Diesel | 7.5–9.0 | $0.14–$0.19 |
| Toyota RAV4 Hybrid | Hybrid | 4.5–5.5 | $0.09–$0.12 |

A hybrid vehicle like the Toyota RAV4 Hybrid delivers fuel cost per km savings of 30–40% compared to its petrol equivalent. Hybrids recapture energy during braking and use electric power at low speeds, which is why they excel in city driving where stop-start traffic would normally waste fuel in a conventional car. On highways, the gap narrows, but hybrids still maintain an advantage on combined cycles.

A typical Australian driver puts between 12,000 and 15,000 km on their car each year. At $0.15/km, that's $1,800–$2,250 annually in fuel alone. A more efficient vehicle or smarter driving habits can easily knock $400–$600 off that bill — money that goes straight back into your pocket rather than into the tank.

Regional drivers face an additional challenge. Fuel in remote areas can cost 20–30 cents more per litre than in major cities, pushing fuel cost per km significantly higher for the same vehicle. If you live in a regional area, your fuel cost per km is one of the strongest arguments for choosing a fuel-efficient model when it's time to replace your car.

## 5 practical tips to reduce fuel costs

You don't need a new car to improve your fuel economy. Small, consistent habits make a real difference at the bowser, and most of them cost nothing to implement.

**1. Check your tyre pressure monthly**

Under-inflated tyres increase rolling resistance, which pushes your L/100km up by 3–5%. That might not sound like much, but over a year of regular driving it adds up to hundreds of kilometres of wasted fuel — and more stops at the pump. Check the recommended pressure on the driver's door placard, not the maximum printed on the tyre sidewall, and check when the tyres are cold for the most accurate reading.

**2. Accelerate smoothly**

Hard acceleration burns extra fuel by demanding more power in a short burst. Aim for gradual throttle inputs and let the car build speed naturally. Over stop-start city driving, smoother inputs can save 10–15% on fuel. Think of it like this: every time you floor it from a set of lights, you're essentially pouring fuel down the exhaust.

**3. Remove unnecessary weight**

Every extra 50 kg in your boot costs roughly 1–2% more fuel. If you're carrying gear, tools, sports equipment, or even a full tank of water you don't need for your regular commute, clearing it out is an instant improvement. It's the easiest way to reduce fuel costs with zero effort and zero cost.

**4. Plan your route and fill-up spots**

Peak-hour traffic and frequent stop-start driving are fuel-intensive. Combining errands into a single trip, travelling outside rush hour, and checking prices before you fill up all add up over time. The [RACV fuel price guide](https://www.racv.com.au/fuel-prices) is a good starting point for finding cheaper stations along your regular routes, especially in metro areas where prices can swing by 20–40 cents per litre between neighbouring suburbs. Some fuel price tracking apps also let you set price alerts so you never miss a cheap day.

**5. Keep your car serviced**

A dirty air filter, worn spark plugs, or old oil all reduce your engine's efficiency. Regular servicing keeps everything running cleanly and helps maintain the L/100km figure your car was designed for. If your fuel cost per km starts creeping up between services, that's often an early warning sign that something needs attention. Catching it early is always cheaper than waiting for a breakdown.
