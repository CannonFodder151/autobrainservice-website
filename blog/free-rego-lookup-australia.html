Free Rego Lookup Australia

AutoBrain Team · 25 August 2026 · Features

Buying a used car? Checking if your rego is current? Need the vehicle details for insurance? A rego lookup takes 10 seconds and could save you thousands.

What is a rego lookup?

A rego lookup checks a vehicle's registration details against the official state or territory transport registry. Enter a licence plate number and get back the current registration status, expiry date, vehicle make and model, engine details, and whether anyone has a financial interest (encumbrance) on the car.

In Australia, each state runs its own registry — VicRoads in Victoria, Service NSW in New South Wales, Department of Transport and Main Roads in Queensland, and so on. AutoBrain's rego lookup tool queries these registries directly, so you get live data without bouncing between state websites.

When to do a rego lookup

  • Before buying a used car. This is the single most important check. It confirms the car's registration is current, reveals any money owing on the vehicle, and verifies the make/model matches what the seller claims. If the seller can't provide a current rego, walk away.
  • Before renewing your own rego. A quick check confirms the expiry date and ensures there are no surprises (like a suspended registration you weren't told about).
  • After an accident. Check the other driver's registration details to confirm they're insured and the vehicle details are correct.
  • For insurance quotes. Insurers ask for registration details — a rego lookup gets you the exact information they need.
  • Car club events. If you're running a cars and coffee or track day, a rego check on every entrant confirms the vehicles are legally registered.

What information does a rego lookup show?

The exact data varies by state, but a standard rego lookup typically returns:

  • Registration status: Current, suspended, cancelled or expired.
  • Expiry date: When the registration needs renewal.
  • Vehicle details: Make, model, year, body type, colour.
  • Engine details: Engine number and type.
  • Encumbrances: Whether a finance company has a security interest over the vehicle (i.e. money owing).
  • Odometer: Some states record the odometer reading from the last inspection.

Using rego lookup when buying a used car

The used car buying process in Australia has a critical gap: there's no national requirement for sellers to provide a vehicle history report. A rego lookup fills the most important part of that gap.

Here's the checklist:

  1. Get the rego number from the seller. If they won't provide it, consider that a red flag.
  2. Run the rego lookup. Check the status, expiry and vehicle details. If the car's registration is expired or suspended, the seller cannot legally sell it for on-road use.
  3. Check encumbrances. If there's a financial interest on the car, the finance company technically owns it until the loan is paid off. Buying a car with an encumbrance means the finance company can repossess it from you — even if you paid the seller in full.
  4. Match the details. Does the make/model/year on the registry match what the seller is showing you? Mismatches can indicate plate swapping or identity issues.
  5. Do the PPSR check. A rego lookup shows encumbrances, but a full Personal Property Securities Register ($2 via the government website) gives you the complete picture.

State-by-state rego check resources

  • Victoria: VicRoads rego check — vicroads.vic.gov.au
  • NSW: Service NSW rego check — service.nsw.gov.au
  • Queensland: TMR rego check — qld.gov.au/transport
  • South Australia: EzyReg — ezyreg.sa.gov.au
  • Western Australia: DoT rego check — dot.wa.gov.au
  • Tasmania: Service Tasmania — service.tas.gov.au
  • NT: MVR rego check — motorvehicle.nt.gov.au
  • ACT: AccessCanberra rego check — accesscanberra.act.gov.au

Common rego lookup red flags

  • Expired registration. The seller may be trying to sell an unregistered car. In most states, you can't transfer an expired registration — the car needs a roadworthy certificate first.
  • Suspended registration. Usually due to uninsured driving or outstanding fines. The car can't be driven until the suspension is lifted.
  • Encumbrances listed. Someone else has a financial claim on the vehicle. Get written proof that the loan is discharged before buying.
  • Vehicle details don't match. If the registry says it's a 2018 Mazda 3 but the seller is showing you a 2020 model, something is wrong.
  • Colour mismatch. Could be innocent (respray) or could indicate plate swapping.
