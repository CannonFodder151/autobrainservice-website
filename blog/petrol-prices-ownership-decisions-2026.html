Are Petrol Prices Making Your Car Too Expensive to Keep?

AutoBrain Team · 3 October 2026 · Ownership Costs

Petrol prices are back on the weekly news cycle, and for a lot of Australians fuel has become the single biggest line item in running a car. So: is your car actually still worth keeping?

How much is petrol really costing you per month?

Most people estimate this badly because they remember the total at the pump, not the cents per kilometre. Work it out properly:

  1. Cost per fill. Note litres and the amount paid at each fill-up.
  2. Kilometres driven. Use your odometer at the start and end of the tank.
  3. Cents per kilometre. (cost ÷ km) × 100.

As a rough benchmark, an average Australian driver doing 2,000 km/month at 10 L/100km and 180 c/L spends around $360/month on fuel. If that number surprises you, tracking it properly is the first fix.

How do I calculate my car's true running costs?

Fuel is only part of it. A car costs money in five places:

  • Fuel — the big variable one.
  • Rego — fixed, but check it.
  • Insurance — usually the second biggest.
  • Servicing — see our service cost guide.
  • Repairs — the wildcard.

Add it up per year, then compare to what a replacement would cost to run. Our real-world fuel consumption guide covers why your real L/100km is usually higher than the brochure figure.

When is it time to replace your car?

Signs it may be time to move on:

  • Repairs are chasing repairs. Multiple unplanned failures in a year, each a preview of the next.
  • Fuel is a fifth of your budget. A thirsty ute or SUV is expensive to run no matter what you do.
  • It's out of warranty and out of fashion — and parts are getting harder to source.
  • Downtime is costing you. A car that strands you has an effective cost higher than its invoice.

Should I upgrade, sell, or keep? A decision framework

The Ownership Advisor runs six modules over your real vehicle data — value, replace, upgrade, finance and more — so the decision rests on numbers:

  • Value — what your car is actually worth today.
  • Replace — the realistic cost of a comparable next car.

If value-per-year says keep and the fuel number is what's hurting, fix the fuel: track prices, fill up cheaper, drive less. If every module says the car has turned expensive to hold, you have your answer.

Frequently asked questions

How much does fuel really cost per month?

Divide your weekly fill-up spend by 4.3, or work out cents-per-kilometre from your odometer. As a guide, 10 L/100km at 2,000 km/month and 180 c/L is about $360/month.

Should I sell my car if fuel is expensive?

Fuel is rarely the reason on its own. Look at total running costs — fuel, rego, insurance, servicing, repairs — and compare against the value of keeping it a year more.
