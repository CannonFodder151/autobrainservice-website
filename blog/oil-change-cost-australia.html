How Much Does an Oil Change Cost in Australia? 2026 Price Guide

AutoBrain Team · 29 August 2026 · Ownership Costs

An oil change is the single most common car service in Australia. Whether you're at a dealer, independent mechanic or doing it yourself, here's what you should expect to pay in 2026 — and how to avoid overpaying.

Oil change cost at a glance (2026 AUD)

Oil typeIndependent mechanicDealerDIY (oil + filter)
Conventional mineral$75–$120$120–$180$45–$70
Semi-synthetic$90–$140$140–$200$55–$85
Full synthetic$110–$175$160–$250$65–$110
Diesel (serviceable filter)$120–$200$180–$300$70–$120

Why prices vary so much

  • Oil type. Full synthetic costs 2–3x more than conventional per litre, but lasts longer and protects better.
  • Vehicle size. A Toyota Corolla takes ~3.7L; a Ford Ranger takes ~7L. More oil = more cost.
  • Filter type. Cartridge filters are cheaper than canister filters. Some cars require OEM filters.
  • Dealer vs independent. Dealers charge a premium for manufacturer-branded oil and factory-trained techs. Independent mechanics use the same (or better) oil for less.
  • Location. Metro Sydney and Melbourne prices run 10–20% higher than regional centres.

Synthetic vs conventional: which should you use?

Check your owner's manual. If it specifies synthetic (most cars made after 2010 do), use synthetic. The extra cost is worth it because:

  • Longer intervals — 12 months / 15,000 km vs 6 months / 10,000 km
  • Better engine protection in Australia's heat
  • Slightly improved fuel economy (1–3%)
  • Reduced engine wear = longer engine life

If your car only needs conventional and you're not driving in extreme conditions, conventional oil is fine. Don't pay for synthetic if you don't need it.

Dealer vs independent: which is better for oil changes?

For a straightforward oil change, an independent mechanic is almost always the better value. They use the same oil specifications, charge less, and often have shorter wait times.

Go to the dealer if:

  • Your car is under warranty and the logbook specifies dealer servicing
  • You want a manufacturer-specific oil grade (e.g. BMW Longlife-01, VW 504/507)
  • You value the dealer stamp in the logbook for resale

How often should you change your oil in Australia?

Australian conditions — heat, dust, stop-start traffic — can be tough on oil. General guidelines:

  • Conventional oil: every 6 months or 10,000 km (whichever first)
  • Full synthetic: every 12 months or 15,000 km (whichever first)
  • Diesel: every 6 months or 10,000 km regardless of oil type

Short trips (under 10 km), heavy towing, and city driving all shorten oil life. If your driving is mostly short urban trips, change every 5,000–7,500 km even with synthetic.

DIY oil change: is it worth it?

If you're comfortable under a car and have the tools, you can save $50–$100 per service. You'll need:

  • Oil (check your manual for type and quantity)
  • Oil filter + new crush washer
  • Drain pan, funnel, socket set, jack and stands
  • ~30 minutes of your time

Important: dispose of used oil at your local council recycling centre — never tip it down the drain.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an oil change cost in Australia?

Expect $75–$175 at an independent mechanic or $120–$250 at a dealer, depending on your car and oil type. DIY costs $45–$110 in materials.

How often should I change my oil in Australia?

Every 6 months or 10,000 km for conventional oil, 12 months / 15,000 km for full synthetic. Australian heat and stop-start traffic can shorten intervals — check your manual.

Is synthetic oil worth the extra cost?

Yes for most modern cars. It lasts longer, protects better in heat, and improves fuel economy slightly. The extra $40–$80 per service is offset by longer intervals.
