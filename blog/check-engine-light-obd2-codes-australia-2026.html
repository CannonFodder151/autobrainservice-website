Check Engine Light On? OBD2 Codes Explained

AutoBrain Team · 3 October 2026 · Diagnostics

The check engine light is the most misunderstood symbol on your dashboard. It is not an emergency — but it is not "ignore it" either.

What does the check engine light actually mean?

Your car's ECU (engine computer) runs hundreds of self-checks. When one fails, it stores an OBD2 fault code and lights the warning symbol. Two rules of thumb:

  • Solid light: fault detected, car usually drivable. Book a mechanic soon.
  • Flashing light: active misfire. Stop driving and recover the car — driving on can destroy the catalytic converter.

For the full dashboard picture, see our car diagnostics guide.

What is OBD2, and how do I read the codes?

OBD2 (On-Board Diagnostics II) is the standard port every Australian car sold since the mid-1990s has — usually under the dash on the driver's side. Plug in a code reader and it shows the stored fault codes.

The 5 most common OBD2 codes and what they cost (AUD)

P0300 — Random/multiple cylinder misfire

Likely causes: worn spark plugs, coil pack, injector. Typical fix: $150–$400. Urgency: high if flashing.

P0420 — Catalyst efficiency below threshold

Likely causes: failing catalytic converter, or an upstream oxygen sensor lying about mix. Typical fix: $300–$1,500 (sensor first — it's cheaper and often the real fault).

P0171 — System too lean

Likely causes: vacuum leak, dirty MAF sensor, weak fuel pump. Typical fix: $100–$600.

P0442 — Evaporative emission system leak (small)

Likely causes: loose or perished fuel cap. Typical fix: $0–$50 — tighten or replace the cap first. This is the classic "light came on after I filled up" code.

P0128 — Coolant thermostat

Likely causes: thermostat stuck open. Typical fix: $150–$350.

Can I clear the code myself?

Yes — any reader can clear codes. But clearing only resets the light. If the fault is still there, the code returns, usually within a few drive cycles. Clear it, drive, and see if it comes back before you spend money. Our check engine light guide covers the reset and drive-cycle reset in detail.

When should I just book a mechanic?

Book a mechanic when the light is flashing, when you have multiple codes, or when a code keeps returning after clearing. If your car won't start at all, that's a different diagnosis — start with our car won't start guide.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to drive with the check engine light on?

A solid light usually means book a mechanic soon. A flashing light means misfiring — stop driving and have the car recovered to protect the catalytic converter.

Can I read OBD2 codes for free?

Yes. Repco and Supercheap Auto often read codes free, and a basic ELM327 adapter plus a free app will read them from your phone.

Will clearing the code fix the problem?

No. Clearing only resets the light. If the fault persists, the code returns — often within a few drive cycles.
