How Much Does Car Service Cost in Australia?

AutoBrain Team · 25 August 2026 · Ownership Costs

If you've ever winced at a mechanic's invoice, you're not alone. The average Australian spends $600–$1,200 per year on car servicing — but knowing what things actually cost helps you budget, compare quotes and avoid overpaying.

Basic logbook service costs

A basic logbook service — oil and filter change, multi-point inspection, fluid top-ups — is the most common maintenance expense. Here's what to expect in 2026:

  • Independent mechanic: $150–$350 AUD. This covers the standard items in your manufacturer's logbook at the 10,000 km or 6-month interval.
  • Dealer service centre: $250–$500 AUD. Dealers use OEM (original equipment manufacturer) parts and factory-trained technicians. The premium is 30–60% over independents.
  • Quick-lube chains (Supercheap Auto, Jax): $120–$250 AUD. Fast, no appointment, but limited to oil/filter — they won't do the full logbook inspection checklist.

The price depends on your car's engine size, oil type (conventional vs synthetic), and whether the manufacturer schedule includes additional items at that interval (e.g. cabin air filter replacement every 20,000 km).

Major service intervals

Beyond the basic 6-month/10,000 km service, major intervals add significant items:

  • 40,000 km / 2 years: Brake fluid replacement ($80–$150), spark plugs on some engines ($100–$300), transmission fluid check. Budget $400–$700 total.
  • 60,000 km / 3 years: Timing belt (if applicable, $500–$1,200), coolant flush ($100–$200), suspension inspection. Budget $700–$1,800.
  • 100,000 km / 5 years: Full timing belt + water pump ($800–$1,500), brake pads and rotors ($400–$800), major fluid changes. Budget $1,500–$3,000.

Not every car needs a timing belt at these intervals — newer engines use timing chains. Check your owner's manual for your specific schedule.

Common repair costs

Beyond scheduled maintenance, here are the most common repairs and what they cost in Australia:

  • Brake pads (per axle): $150–$350 parts + labour. Rotors add $200–$500 per axle if needed.
  • Clutch replacement: $800–$2,000 depending on the car. labour-intensive job (4–6 hours).
  • Battery replacement: $150–$400. Start-stop batteries (AGM/EFB) are at the higher end.
  • Alternator: $400–$800 including labour.
  • Water pump: $300–$700. Often done with the timing belt for shared labour.
  • O2 sensor: $200–$500. Common trigger for P0420 check engine light.
  • Catalytic converter: $1,000–$3,000. One of the most expensive common repairs — and a theft target.

Dealer vs independent: when each makes sense

Use the dealer when:

  • The car is under warranty — dealer stamps in the logbook remove any warranty dispute risk.
  • The repair involves emissions or drivetrain components covered by an extended warranty.
  • Your car requires specialist diagnostic equipment (e.g. European luxury brands).

Use an independent mechanic when:

  • The car is out of warranty — you'll save 30–60% on the same work.
  • You want flexibility on parts choice (quality aftermarket parts cost less than OEM).
  • You've found a mechanic you trust — word of mouth and online reviews matter more than a brand badge.

How to reduce your service costs

  1. Track everything. A digital logbook like AutoBrain shows your total spend per vehicle, cost per service, and when upcoming maintenance is due. Knowledge is leverage when comparing quotes.
  2. Get three quotes. For any repair over $300, get quotes from two independents and one dealer. You'll quickly see the price range.
  3. Don't over-service. Follow the manufacturer's schedule, not the workshop's upsell. If the manual says 15,000 km, you don't need a service at 8,000 km.
  4. Use quality aftermarket parts. Brands like Bendix, Ryco and Bosch make parts that meet or exceed OEM specifications at lower prices.
  5. Bundle work. If you need brakes and a service, do them at the same time — you save on duplicate labour charges.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a basic car service cost in Australia?

A basic logbook service (oil, filter, inspection) costs $150–$350 AUD at an independent mechanic and $250–$500 at a dealer. The price depends on the car's make, the oil type required, and whether the manufacturer schedule includes additional checks at that interval.

How often should I service my car?

Most manufacturers recommend every 6 months or 10,000 km, whichever comes first. Some newer cars with synthetic oil go to 12 months or 15,000 km. Check your owner's manual — the manufacturer's schedule is what matters for warranty.

Is dealer servicing more expensive?

Yes, typically 30–60% more. Dealers use OEM parts and factory-trained technicians, which justifies the premium for warranty work. Independent mechanics use equivalent-quality aftermarket parts and charge less labour. For out-of-warranty cars, an independent mechanic is usually the better value.

How much do brakes cost to replace?

Front brake pads: $150–$350 per axle. Rear brake pads: $120–$300 per axle. Rotors (discs): $200–$500 per axle if they need replacement. A full front brake job (pads + rotors) typically costs $400–$800 at an independent mechanic.
