What is a car service log book?

A car service log book is the chronological record of every service, repair, inspection and modification done to a vehicle. In Australia it doubles as a resale document — buyers expect to see it, mechanics check it, and the ATO references it when you claim work-related vehicle expenses. A complete log book records the date, odometer reading, work performed, parts replaced, workshop name, and cost.

ATO requirements for logbooks

The Australian Taxation Office requires a valid logbook if you claim a deduction for work-related car expenses. The logbook method requires you to record every trip for a continuous 12-week period, then apply that percentage across the entire financial year. For work-related expenses specifically, the ATO requires:

  • Start and end odometer readings for each trip.
  • Date of each trip.
  • Purpose of the trip (business or private).
  • Distance travelled for each trip.

Beyond the tax office, a complete service logbook is also required to:

  • Maintain warranty coverage under Australian Consumer Law.
  • Prove you followed the manufacturer's service schedule when selling.
  • Keep resale value — vehicles with no service history sell for 10-15% less.

Recommended service intervals Australia

Service intervals in Australia follow one of two patterns — time-based (every 12 months regardless of distance) or distance-based (every 10,000–15,000 km for modern cars, 5,000–7,500 km for older vehicles). Most manufacturers use a "whichever comes first" rule. Check your owner's manual for the exact interval for your vehicle.

Typical service interval schedule

  • Oil & filter: Every 10,000 km or 12 months (synthetic oil).
  • Minor service: Every 15,000 km or 12 months — oil, filter, safety check.
  • Major service: Every 30,000 km or 24 months — plugs, fluids, belts, brakes.
  • Brake fluid: Every 24 months regardless of distance.
  • Coolant: Every 60,000 km or 36 months (check manual).
  • Timing belt: Every 120,000–180,000 km or 10 years (critical).
  • Tyres: Rotate every 10,000 km, replace when tread reaches 1.6 mm.

Australian conditions — heat, dust, salt air in coastal areas — can shorten these intervals. Regional and rural drivers should treat the lower end of each range as their standard.

What to record (every single time)

A useful logbook entry has more than a sticker. Record:

  1. Date — when the work was done.
  2. Odometer reading — current km before the car left the workshop.
  3. Workshop name & mechanic — ABN if claiming tax.
  4. Work performed — service type, parts replaced, fluid top-ups.
  5. Invoice number — cross-reference with receipts.
  6. Next service due — date or km, whichever comes first.
  7. Photos — engine bay, service book stamp, receipts.

If you're claiming the car as a tax deduction, the ATO also wants the business-use percentage and trip records kept separately from service records.

Paper logbook vs digital logbook

Paper logbooks are the traditional choice — the physical book that comes with every new car in Australia. They get lost, water-damaged or filled up. Digital logbooks solve those problems. The ATO accepts digital records as long as they are a true and accurate representation of the original entry and can't be easily altered after the fact.

A digital car service logbook gives you searchable history, photo attachments, automated reminders for upcoming service intervals, and resale-ready export. The extra storage is free — the only cost is the five seconds it takes to enter each record.

FAQ

Is a digital logbook valid in Australia?

Yes. The ATO accepts digital records as long as they accurately represent the original entry and can't be tampered with after creation. AutoBrain's timestamped, exportable records meet this requirement.

How often should I service my car in Australia?

Most modern cars follow a 12-month / 15,000 km interval, whichever comes first. Check your owner's manual for your specific vehicle. Older vehicles and those driven in coastal or dusty conditions should use the lower end of each interval.

Can I claim car services on tax in Australia?

If you use your car for work, yes — but only the work-related portion. You need a valid logbook and receipts for all claimed expenses. The logbook method (12 weeks of trip records) or cents-per-kilometre method both require accurate records.

What happens if I lose my service logbook?

A lost paper logbook can be reconstructed from workshop invoices and receipts. A digital logbook that's backed up regularly never has this problem. AutoBrain exports your full history as JSON so you always have a copy.
