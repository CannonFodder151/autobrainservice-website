Car Fuel Tracker Australia

AutoBrain Team · 25 August 2026 · Fuel & Cost Tracking

Every fill-up tells a story. How much you're spending, how efficiently you're driving, and whether that new route to work is actually saving you money — or costing you more.

Why track your fuel costs?

Fuel is the second-largest ongoing cost of car ownership in Australia (after insurance). The average Australian driver spends $2,500–$4,000 per year on fuel — and most have no idea what their real cost per kilometre is. They know what they pay at the bowser, but not whether their fuel economy is improving or degrading over time.

A fuel tracker changes that. By logging every fill-up, you can see:

  • Your actual L/100km — not the trip computer's optimistic number, but the real figure based on litres pumped and kilometres driven.
  • Cost per kilometre — so you can compare vehicles, routes, and driving styles with a single metric.
  • Trends over time — is your fuel economy getting worse? That could indicate a maintenance issue (dirty air filter, low tyre pressure, worn spark plugs).
  • Budget forecasting — know exactly what you'll spend on fuel next month based on your actual usage pattern.

How to calculate fuel cost per km

The formula is simple:

Cost per km = Total fuel cost ÷ Total kilometres driven

For example, if you spent $420 on fuel last month and drove 2,100 km, your cost per km is $0.20. That's $0.20 of fuel for every kilometre you drove.

For L/100km (the standard Australian measure):

L/100km = (Litres pumped ÷ Kilometres driven) × 100

If you pumped 85 litres and drove 620 km: (85 ÷ 620) × 100 = 13.7 L/100km.

Average fuel costs in Australia (2026)

Here's what Australian drivers are paying per kilometre across different vehicle types, based on current fuel prices (approximately $2.10/L for unleaded 91):

  • Small hatchback (Toyota Corolla, Mazda 3): 5.5–7.0 L/100km → $0.12–$0.15/km
  • Medium sedan (Toyota Camry, Mazda 6): 7.0–8.5 L/100km → $0.15–$0.18/km
  • Small SUV (Toyota RAV4, Mazda CX-5): 7.5–9.0 L/100km → $0.16–$0.19/km
  • Large SUV (Toyota Prado, Ford Everest): 9.0–12.0 L/100km → $0.19–$0.25/km
  • Ute (Ford Ranger, Toyota HiLux): 8.5–12.0 L/100km → $0.18–$0.25/km
  • Diesel vehicles: typically 10–15% better L/100km than petrol equivalents, but diesel costs ~$0.15–$0.20 more per litre, so per-km cost is often similar.

Fuel tracking for tax deductions

If you use your personal vehicle for work (not commuting to your regular workplace), you can claim the business-use portion of fuel as a tax deduction. The ATO requires a detailed log with:

  • Date of each fill-up
  • Odometer reading
  • Litres and cost
  • Fuel type
  • Business vs. personal kilometres

AutoBrain tracks all of this automatically. At tax time, export your fuel history as CSV and give it to your accountant. No more shoebox of receipts.

The ATO cents-per-kilometre method (85 cents/km for 2025–26) is simpler, but if you drive a lot of business kilometres, the logbook method with actual fuel costs often gives a larger deduction. A fuel tracker makes the logbook method effortless.

Tips to improve your fuel economy

  • Check tyre pressure monthly. Under-inflated tyres increase fuel consumption by 3–5%. Check the placard on the driver's door jamb, not the number on the tyre sidewall.
  • Remove unnecessary weight. Every 50 kg of extra weight costs about 1–2% more fuel.
  • Drive smoothly. Accelerate gently, brake early, and maintain a steady speed. Aggressive driving wastes 15–30% more fuel.
  • Use cruise control on highways. It maintains a steady speed and avoids the acceleration-deceleration cycle that burns fuel.
  • Servicing matters. A dirty air filter, old spark plugs, or degraded oil all increase fuel consumption. Track your service schedule in AutoBrain.
  • Compare fuel prices. Use apps like FuelMap Australia or Petrol Spy to find the cheapest fuel near you. The price difference between stations can be 20–40 cents per litre.
