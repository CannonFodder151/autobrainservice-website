OBD2 Code Reader App Australia

AutoBrain Team · 25 August 2026 · Diagnostics

Your check engine light is on. Before you panic — or pay a workshop $150 just to tell you what the code means — grab your phone and an OBD2 adapter.

What is an OBD2 code reader?

Every car sold in Australia since 2006 has an OBD2 port — a 16-pin diagnostic socket usually hidden under the dashboard. A Bluetooth ELM327 adapter (about $15–$40 AUD on Amazon AU or eBay) plugs into that port and talks to your car's engine computer. An OBD2 code reader app on your phone then reads the diagnostic trouble codes (DTCs) stored by the ECU.

When the check engine light comes on, the car's computer stores a code like P0300 (random misfire) or P0420 (catalytic converter efficiency below threshold). Without a reader, you're guessing. With one, you know exactly what the car is complaining about — and whether it's safe to keep driving.

How to read OBD2 codes on your phone

  1. Get an ELM327 adapter. Bluetooth or USB — both work. For Android, Bluetooth is easiest. For iPhone, a Lightning or USB-C ELM327 adapter works with iOS.
  2. Plug it in. Find the OBD2 port (usually under the steering column, sometimes behind a small panel). Push the adapter in until it clicks.
  3. Pair with your phone. Open Bluetooth settings, find the ELM327 device, and pair. Some adapters have a PIN (usually 1234 or 0000).
  4. Open AutoBrain. Go to the Diagnostics section, tap "Scan Codes." The app connects to the adapter, queries the ECU, and shows every stored code.
  5. Read the explanation. AutoBrain doesn't just show "P0300" — it tells you in plain English what the code means, what likely caused it, how serious it is, and what the repair typically costs in Australia.

The most common OBD2 codes in Australia

After scanning thousands of vehicles across Australia, these are the codes our users see most often:

  • P0300 — Random/Multiple Cylinder Misfire. The engine is misfiring across multiple cylinders. Common causes: worn spark plugs, ignition coil failure, vacuum leaks, or fuel delivery issues. Repair cost: $200–$800 depending on cause.
  • P0420 — Catalyst System Efficiency Below Threshold (Bank 1). The catalytic converter isn't cleaning exhaust gases effectively. Could be a failing cat, a faulty O2 sensor, or an engine issue upstream. Repair cost: $400–$2,500 depending on whether it's the sensor or the converter.
  • P0171 — System Too Lean (Bank 1). Too much air or not enough fuel reaching the engine. Check for vacuum leaks, a dirty mass airflow sensor, or weak fuel pump. Repair cost: $100–$600.
  • P0440 — Evaporative Emission Control System Malfunction. Usually a loose or faulty fuel cap, cracked hose, or charcoal canister issue. Start with the $5 fuel cap replacement. Repair cost: $5–$500.
  • P0128 — Coolant Thermostat Below Thermostat Regulating Temperature. The engine isn't reaching operating temperature fast enough. Typically a stuck-open thermostat. Repair cost: $150–$400.

Which ELM327 adapter should I buy?

Any ELM327 v2.1+ adapter works with AutoBrain. Popular options in Australia:

  • Veepeak OBDCheck BLE+ — reliable Bluetooth, works with iOS and Android (~$35 AUD).
  • OBDLink MX+ — premium option with faster readings and better compatibility (~$70 AUD).
  • Generic ELM327 Bluetooth — $15–$20 on eBay, works fine for basic code reading.

Avoid the cheapest no-name adapters — they often use counterfeit chips that drop connections mid-scan. If your adapter keeps disconnecting, try a different one before blaming the app.

When to see a mechanic instead of just clearing codes

An OBD2 code reader is a diagnostic tool, not a repair tool. Clearing a code turns off the check engine light, but if the underlying fault hasn't been fixed, the light will come back. See a mechanic when:

  • The code is related to emissions (P04xx) and you're about to get a VictorianRoadworthy or interstate equivalent inspection.
  • The check engine light is flashing — that's an active misfire that can damage the catalytic converter if you keep driving.
  • Multiple codes appear at once, suggesting a systemic issue.
  • You've cleared the code twice and it comes back within a few drive cycles.

Frequently asked questions

What is an OBD2 code reader?

An OBD2 code reader connects to your car's OBD2 port and reads diagnostic trouble codes stored by the engine computer. These codes tell you what triggered the check engine light — for example P0300 means random misfire, P0420 means catalytic converter efficiency below threshold.

Do I need a special adapter?

Yes — you need an ELM327-compatible Bluetooth or USB adapter that plugs into your car's OBD2 port. These cost $15–$40 AUD online. Once paired with your phone, an OBD2 app like AutoBrain can read and explain every code in plain English.

Can I clear the check engine light?

Yes, most OBD2 apps let you clear codes, which turns off the check engine light. However, clearing a code does not fix the underlying problem — the light will come back if the fault persists. Always diagnose first, fix the issue, then clear.

What are the most common OBD2 codes?

The most common codes are P0300 (random misfire), P0420 (catalytic converter efficiency), P0171 (system too lean), P0440 (evaporative emission system), and P0128 (coolant thermostat). AutoBrain explains each code and estimates the typical repair cost in Australian dollars.
