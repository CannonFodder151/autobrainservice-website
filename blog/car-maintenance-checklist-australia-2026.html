Car Maintenance Checklist Australia 2026 Guide

AutoBrain Team · 17 September 2026 · Maintenance

Keep your vehicle roadworthy and your wallet happy with this complete car maintenance checklist for Australian drivers. From logbook service intervals to seasonal safety checks, we've got you covered.

Logbook Service Intervals

The gold standard for vehicle maintenance in Australia is following your manufacturer's logbook schedule. Missing an interval can void warranty coverage and lead to costly repairs down the track.

  • 10,000 km / 6 months: Basic logbook service — oil and filter change, multi-point inspection, fluid top-ups.
  • 15,000 km / 6 months: Intermediate service — adds cabin air filter, brake fluid check, tyre rotation.
  • 20,000 km / 12 months: Major service — includes spark plug replacement (if applicable), transmission fluid inspection, coolant system flush.
  • 30,000 km / 18–24 months: Comprehensive inspection — brake pads/shoes, suspension components, steering linkage, exhaust system.
  • 40,000 km / 30 months: Timing belt inspection (if applicable), brake fluid replacement, transmission service.

Pro tip: Not every car needs a timing belt — newer engines use timing chains. Check your owner's manual for your specific schedule.

Seasonal Maintenance Checklist

Summer (December–February)

  • Check coolant level and concentration — prevent overheating in Australian heat.
  • Inspect tyre pressure and tread depth — hot roads increase pressure and wear.
  • Test air conditioning system — ensure it blows cold before the peak summer.
  • Check battery health — heat accelerates battery degradation.

Winter (June–August)

  • Inspect windscreen wipers and washer fluid — winter rains require good visibility.
  • Check battery voltage — cold temperatures reduce battery capacity.
  • Inspect brakes before wet season — increased stopping distances.
  • Verify all lights are functioning — shorter daylight hours increase nighttime driving.

DIY Checks You Can Do Monthly

  1. Tyres: Pressure (check when cold), tread depth (minimum 1.5mm legal in Australia), look for cuts or embedded objects.
