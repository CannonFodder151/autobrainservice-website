Fair Price for a Used Car in Australia 2026

AutoBrain Team · 1 October 2026 · Ownership Costs

The difference between a fair deal and overpaying by $3,000 comes down to research — not luck. Here's how to value any used car in Australia, spot the traps and negotiate from a position of knowledge.

Where to find real market values

Don't trust the asking price. Cross-reference these three sources for every car you're serious about:

  • RedBook (redbook.com.au): Industry-standard wholesale and retail ranges. Free tier gives a band — use the midpoint as your anchor.
  • CarsGuide / Drive valuations: Similar methodology, sometimes different by 5-10%. Average the two if they diverge.
  • Live listings (Carsales, Facebook Marketplace, Gumtree): Filter by identical make/model/year/variant, then by km (±15,000 km), condition and state. The median of 10+ comparable listings is your real market.

Pro tip: RedBook retail = dealer forecourt price. RedBook trade = what a dealer pays at auction. A fair private-sale price sits roughly halfway between trade and retail.

Negotiation checklist — do this before you make an offer

  1. Run the PPSR check ($2 at ppsr.gov.au) — confirms no finance, not stolen, not written off.
  2. Book a pre-purchase inspection ($200-350) — mobile inspector comes to the seller. Non-negotiable for cars >$5k.
  3. Calculate your walk-away number = median comparable - red flag deductions - $500 buffer.
  4. Open with your walk-away minus $500-1,000 — gives room to move without exceeding your limit.
  5. Use the inspection report as leverage — every defect found is a line-item deduction, not a vague "bit less".
  6. Factor in on-road costs — stamp duty (3-5% of market value), transfer fee (~$30), rego if due.

Final rule: never fall in love before the inspection

The car that "feels right" on the test drive is the one that makes you skip the PPI. That's how $5,000 mistakes happen. Treat every viewing as data collection — the inspection report is your negotiation document, the PPSR is your legal shield, and the valuation is your anchor. Walk away from any seller who pressures you to skip steps.
