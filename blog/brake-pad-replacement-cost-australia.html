Brake Pad Replacement Cost Australia 2026

AutoBrain Team · 21 September 2026 · Maintenance

What brake pads cost in Australia, how often you need them, and the difference between cheap and good.

Front Brake Pad Replacement Cost

Front brake pads wear faster than rears because they handle 60–70% of braking force. Expect to pay:

  • Parts only: $100–$300 for a pair of front pads
  • Installed at independent mechanic: $200–$400 total
  • Installed at dealer: $350–$600 total

Prices vary by vehicle — a small hatchback like a Toyota Corolla costs less than an SUV or ute. Premium ceramic pads sit at the higher end.

Rear Brake Pad Replacement Cost

Rear pads last longer and cost slightly less:

  • Parts only: $80–$250 for a pair
  • Installed at independent mechanic: $180–$350 total
  • Installed at dealer: $300–$500 total

Ceramic vs Semi-Metallic vs Organic

Type Cost per Set Lifespan Pros Cons
Semi-metallic $60–$120 30,000–50,000 km Cheap, good heat tolerance Noisy, dusty
Ceramic $100–$200 50,000–70,000 km Quiet, low dust, long life Higher upfront cost
Organic $40–$80 20,000–40,000 km Cheapest, quiet Wears fast, poor heat resistance

For Australian conditions — heat, hills, stop-start traffic — ceramic pads are usually the best value over time.

When to Replace Your Brake Pads

Watch for these signs:

  • Squealing or grinding noise when braking — the wear indicator is telling you something
  • Longer stopping distances — if your car takes more room to stop, pads are thin
  • Brake pedal feels soft or spongy — could be pads or fluid
  • Car pulls to one side when braking — uneven pad wear
  • Visual check — if pad material is less than 3mm, replace soon

Don't Forget the Rotors

Brake pads and rotors work together. If you ignore worn pads, you'll score the rotor surface and need to replace the disc too — that's an extra $200–$500 per rotor. A workshop can measure rotor thickness and advise whether they're still within spec.

DIY vs Workshop

Replacing brake pads is one of the simpler maintenance jobs, but there are risks if you're not confident:

  • DIY: Save $100–$200 on labour. You'll need a jack, axle stands, and basic tools. Budget 1–2 hours per axle.
  • Workshop: They'll inspect rotors, calipers and brake fluid while they're in there. A good mechanic will flag issues you'd miss.

If you're not sure, pay the labour — brakes are not the place to learn on the job.

Budget $200–$400 for a front brake pad replacement at an independent mechanic, or $350–$600 at a dealer. Go ceramic if you can — they last longer and your wheels stay cleaner.
